About

A career at the intersection of health information, editorial excellence, and digital transformation.

Susanna Guzman is a content strategist, editor, and digital transformation leader with more than 30 years of experience in health information, medical publishing, and membership association management.

Much of her career has been spent inside health-focused membership associations and nonprofits — organizations where content must serve clinicians, members, patients, and the public simultaneously, often across journals, digital platforms, CME programs, and consumer health resources at once. That context demands a rare combination of editorial rigor, information architecture, governance discipline, and cross-functional leadership.

She has served as Director of Content & Digital Optimization at AAFP (leading a team of ~15 across aafp.org and familydoctor.org), Director of Web Services at CFA Institute (leading a $4M enterprise content transformation), and Director of Consumer Health Information at March of Dimes. She has also consulted for Mayo Clinic, ZS Associates, and Fortune 500 companies on content strategy, health literacy, and digital governance.

She holds certifications in accessibility (IAAP) and health literacy (IHA), and believes that well-governed, structured, accessible information is the foundation of every meaningful health outcome.

Her practice is grounded in behavioral and social science research — including Aron's self-expansion theory, Gottman's relationship research, and conversational UX theory — as well as original methodology: a content evaluation framework synthesizing WCAG, CDC health literacy, Prochaska's Transtheoretical Model, and Bloom's Taxonomy, operationalized as an AI agent workflow.

Point of View

How I think about health information, content, and the people who depend on both.

Health information management and content strategy are converging. The organizations that thrive will be the ones that treat information governance not as a compliance exercise, but as the foundation of every member, patient, and practitioner relationship they want to build.

01

Information governance is not a compliance exercise

Governance done right is the infrastructure that makes every member touchpoint consistent, trustworthy, and scalable. It defines how an organization speaks — and whether its audiences can depend on what it says.

02

Associations have the hardest content problem

A membership association must serve credentialed professionals, students, employers, patients, policymakers, and the public — often on the same platform. That demands audience segmentation, personalization, and omnichannel discipline that most commercial organizations never have to attempt.

03

Publications are a strategic asset, not a legacy obligation

Peer-reviewed journals and industry publications are among the most credible content an association produces. Managed well — with strong editorial standards, author development, and integration into digital channels — they drive thought leadership, membership value, and revenue.

04

Accessibility is not optional in health information

71% of people with disabilities leave a non-accessible site immediately. In health information, inaccessible content isn't just a legal risk — it's a failure of the mission. Certified in accessibility (IAAP) and health literacy (IHA), I build this in from the start.

05

Content strategy and IT must speak the same language

The gap between content vision and technical execution is where transformation initiatives fail. Closing it requires a strategist who can work fluently with IT, CMS platforms, data systems, and cross-functional teams — not just hand off a brief and wait.

06

Data-informed doesn't mean data-driven

Metrics tell you what happened. Strategy tells you what it means and what to do next. The most effective content leaders use data to pressure-test decisions — not to replace judgment, editorial instincts, or the mission-driven values that give an organization its credibility.
